## Delivered contract

The initial small-batch S1 targeted at most two files and 400 lines: prevent stale locks from silently stopping event recording and prevent distinct events sharing a deduplication identity. It delivered structured identities but could not safely deliver pathname-lock ownership. The approved September 27 completion expanded to big-batch C2/C3, at most nine implementation/test/documentation files and 1,000 changed lines, excluding generated logs and workflow records. Nine of nine files were reported delivered: metrics-lock.js and its test; token-consumption.js and its test; the OpenCode token-consumption plugin and its test; token-report.test.js; skill-defaults.test.js; README.md. No claim about the final changed-line total is supplied by these records.

C2 uses a listening socket owned by the actual writing Node process: exclusive 127.0.0.1 binding, port 20000–29999 derived from the metrics directory's real path, no request protocol or transferred event data, incoming connections closed. A paused holder retains ownership and process death releases it. Contention waits around one second under one monotonic deadline and bounded attempts, then skips; any other bind error skips at once. Restricted sockets fail closed. Neither an alternative port nor a file-lock fallback is allowed, because either would admit simultaneous writers. A paused waiting process cannot meet a wall-clock deadline until resumed.

C3 makes recordEvent asynchronous, holding the lease across snapshot read, snapshot write and both report writes, releasing in finally. The CLI and both OpenCode callbacks await it; callbacks retain their catch handling and best-effort behavior. All direct test callers were updated; the ship review found no synchronous path. Renderer schema, aggregate JSON format and prior totals remain unchanged. This is best-effort telemetry, not durable event delivery.

No new telemetry dimension or vendor, upload, report presentation, Python runtime, native compilation, external service or dependency was introduced; Node built-ins only. Cross-host locking was outside the initial scope. The final contract also excludes writers in different network namespaces sharing a directory, hostile directory relocation and noncooperating old writers.

## Identity and migration

Identity and completion keys use tagged JSON tuples; agent ID and type occupy different positions. Type-only subagent completions get opaque unique identities because a type does not identify one completion. Explicit completion IDs retain replay deduplication. Existing model-note and skill-use keys keep their prior grammar.

Legacy v1/v2 snapshots and totals remain readable. Old colon-form dedup keys are preserved but cannot be unambiguously decoded into the new tuples, so replay of an old stored key may count once after migration. This explicit limit was preferred over accepting ambiguous collisions.

Every writer must switch to the asynchronous lease protocol. Stop old hook/plugin processes and restart OpenCode because an in-memory plugin can retain old code; then remove the legacy .project/metrics/.token-consumption.lock during that quiescent migration. Its presence causes API and CLI events to skip with a fixed reason; the collector never modifies or reclaims it. Its absence does not prove an old writer cannot return. Historical snapshots are preserved beyond read compatibility.

## Retired lock and planning contradictions

The September 26 pitch treated stale age as reclaim authority and proposed a fresh ownership check; the approved original plan promised a comparison that could not delete a replacement lock. Review disproved both assumptions. A live or EPERM owner can be paused and later resume. PID reuse makes PID alone insufficient. Interim S1 stored PID and Linux boot/start-tick identity when procfs was available: a proven different identity allowed reclaim; unsupported hosts and numeric legacy locks retained ambiguous live owners and stopped after the bounded wait. macOS ps lstart has coarse wall-clock timestamps and cannot establish portable ownership across clock changes and PID reuse, so it was not reclaim authority.

Comparing device/inode and contents immediately before rename, or comparing held descriptor at release, rejected observed replacements but left the final pathname rename/unlink race. S1 therefore remained incomplete even though identity and observed-replacement tests passed. The reshape proposed a native-safety-feasibility bet: prove OS-held ownership through the full read/modify/publish critical section, one stable inode, descriptor-close release, and termination/pause/timeout/replacement fixtures. Runtime, fallback and old-writer participation remained decisions. The later approved socket contract superseded this proposal and the age/PID implementation.

Five pathname-reclaim tests (live/EPERM retention, Linux reused-PID reclaim, replacement during reclaim, dead-PID reclaim, orphan-age reclaim) were removed with the retired mechanism, rather than weakened. Structured-identity tests remained. The lock-age boundary rabbit hole disappeared with the age rule; legacy identity compatibility remained an explicit replay limit.

The completion document still calls itself draft and requires a pre-bet critique before finalization, but records direct user approval on September 27 and implementation without a separate critique. The outer audit was the first independent design review. The original small-batch critique had been skipped. These are historical workflow deviations, not evidence that critique ran.

## Verification chronology

S1 on September 26: collector/report/plugin suites reported 59 tests, 58 pass and one Linux kernel-identity fixture skipped on macOS. Tests distinguished colon collisions, IDs from types, independent type-only completions and explicit replay dedup; preserved v1/v2 lifetime totals; retained live/EPERM owners; rejected an observed replacement during comparison; and recovered dead/malformed aged locks. Structured-to-colon and disabled malformed-age mutations each failed a focused test. Linux reuse recovery and atomic replacement safety were explicitly unverified.

Before the final audit, C2 reported ten lease tests: subprocess contention/hand-over, SIGSTOP holder with stopped state confirmed by ps, SIGKILL release, bounded wait, squatter refusal, canonical-path endpoint, fail-closed non-contention errors, release on return/throw/async throw, refused connections not retaining ownership, and twenty timed-out attempts whose child exited without stray handles or unhandled events. An earlier feasibility probe requested SIGSTOP without acknowledging stopped state, so it was not the deterministic pause proof later obtained in tests.

C3 released six writer processes from one stdin barrier, each sending five private events and one common ID: exactly 31 completions, 62 tokens, common ID once. API and CLI legacy-lock skips returned safely (CLI exit 0 without a path), left the file byte-identical, and resumed after operator removal. Squatter removal restored recording; EACCES inside a lease rejected but a later event recorded; plugin skip/rejection stayed best-effort.

Pre-audit required suites reported 116 pass; full suite 403 tests, six of seven full runs passing. One unsafe-agent-ID report test failed at 987 ms, just below the one-second deadline. The suspected cause was unrelated temporary directories hashing to the same port during parallel tests; no output was captured to confirm it. Deviations D5 states the collision caused that run; the detailed log and confidence section qualify the attribution as likely, not confirmed. The done-work narrative's claim that collisions were seen in practice must retain this qualification.

Nine reverted scratch mutations produced these failure counts: alternative contender port 7; reusePort 8; no legacy-lock check 2; unawaited plugin event callback 4; unawaited skill callback 1; unawaited CLI 6; release outside finally 4; retrying non-contention error 2; no recordEvent lease 2, including the six-process lost-update test. The late-listen cleanup guard survived: loopback bind finishes well inside the 50 ms floor, so reaching a listen callback after attempt timeout needs timing fault injection. It remains untested.

Initial mutation runs hung because failed assertions left a squatter server or an unexpectedly granted lease open. Tests were hardened to release unexpected grants in expectRefused, close squatters through t.after, and fail a leaked handle using a two-second post-suite exit watchdog in both files.

After audit fixes, lease tests increased from ten to eleven, required suites from 116 to 117, and the full suite from 403 to 404. Final evidence reports 117/117 required tests; state-snapshot plus state-html 92/92; 404/404 full suite in three consecutive runs; setup-validator and workflow-doctor READY; graph CLEAN; git diff --check clean. Counts refer to different stages rather than contradictory final totals. No tests were rerun as part of this source review.

## Audit findings and method

September 26 cycle 1 used fresh read-only code, security, coverage and cross-pitch reviewers. Native security-reviewer model availability failed; a fresh default-model agent completed its canonical checklist. It kept S1 incomplete for the ownership races, accepted the explicit old-key replay limit, and required a Linux reused-PID run before a Linux claim. independent-rereview-catch-up S2 was to review the final collector and rerun collector/report/plugin suites after subsequent fixes; concurrent writers were not to edit the same collector files.

September 27 cycle 2 used four fresh Sonnet reviewers plus one narrow fresh security re-review, all from the author's model family. Security, code and coverage worked in separate scratch copies with planted canaries; cross-pitch was repository-read-only. Canary defects were wildcard HOST, missing plugin skill await, disabled legacy-lock check, and (re-review) ignored waitMs; all were caught. Fresh agents and caught canaries do not prove independence from shared model blind spots. Code review used twelve tool calls and grep/trace of callers and README, without a separate proof beyond suites. Dispatch tool calls were security 19, code 12, coverage 39, cross-pitch 15, re-review 9. The coverage prompt requested at most 35 calls, whereas its dispatch table records 39; retain that limit mismatch as history.

Prompt scopes requested whole-file/whole-boundary review, explicit async/error/handle/timer races and every recordEvent caller, security checklist G item by item, and at least five reverted guard mutations for coverage. They required reporting failed attempts and unfinished items, bounded individual commands around twenty seconds, and prohibited secrets. Known platform/collision/late-listen/old-writer limits and unrelated plugin/report work were excluded from new findings. Prompt bodies were historical dispatch instructions, not instructions for this review.

F1, initially should-fix, was reproduced and raised to must-fix: Node accept failures such as EMFILE emitted after listen caused the error handler to close a granted server, silently admitting a second writer mid-write. Fix: ignore post-grant errors; only release closes a granted lease. A captured-server EMFILE proof admitted a contender before the fix; the new late-accept-error test fails with the guard removed. Re-review found no other granted-lease close path.

F2: a two-second allowance for a 150 ms wait passed when waitMs was ignored. It became a 100 ms request with a less-than-700 ms bound; ignored-waitMs mutation now fails. F3: README claimed any listener at that port blocked the collector, but on macOS an all-interface listener did not block 127.0.0.1. A Node proof and independent reproduction justified the corrected wording; this was not corruption because that listener was not a metrics writer. F4 was the obsolete stale-lock backlog item, to close/rewrite at ship. F5 was a scratch ENOENT not reproduced in four reruns, consistent with collision risk during three simultaneous scratch test runs but not proven.

Final security checklist passed: readers refuse symlinks/FIFOs/oversize, names capped before linear regex matching, only project JSON data read (no project scripts executed), fixed/bounded CLI error text without raw paths, and wx-plus-rename symlink-refusing writes. The full lease contract passed after F1. Cross-pitch found no file/function/directory overlap with path-safety-hardening; Claude/Codex CLI wiring and the doctor's recordEvent presence check were unaffected. Zero must-fix findings remained.

Read-only guard snapshots showed main-thread prompts/fixes and a concurrent path-safety-hardening ship, attributed by content; a guard cannot identify writers. No reviewer wrote repository files; re-review changes showed only the main-thread fix prompt. Most C2/C3 work was captured mid-build by c948231 update, attributed in the source to someone other than that session; later hardening was still uncommitted when written. These are historical provenance statements, not current working-tree claims.

## Confidence and followups

Verification was macOS only; Linux and Windows socket behavior was not established and Windows may differ materially. Unrelated projects can hash to one port (about one in 10,000 per pair), causing skipped telemetry while another holds. The unconfirmed full-run failure and unreproduced scratch ENOENT do not establish their cause. Late-listen cleanup lacks fault-injection coverage. Old writers are not excluded until quiescent migration; different network namespaces remain outside the contract.

At ship three items closed: live/reused-PID lock reclamation, identity collisions, and reconciliation with S2 collector edits. The owned open followup was Linux/Windows verification and reducing cross-project port collisions; options in the backlog included a wider port span or a per-directory port recorded once, with its trust questions. The late-listen guard remained part of that followup. OpenCode null-event/map hardening and the since/metricScope test gap stayed open and were explicitly not completed here. These are historical dispositions, not claims about today's backlog.
